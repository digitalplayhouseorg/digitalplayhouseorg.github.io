00Abstract

Abstract

The Open Learning Protocol (OLP) is a decentralised learning protocol: every participant holds a Nostr keypair identity, every learner keeps a Git repository as an immutable, verifiable learning record, payments run on the Bitcoin Lightning Network, and governance runs through School Improvement Proposals and a multi-signature Council. CREDU is a practitioner-sovereign credentialing protocol: a federated registry of professional graphs hosted on infrastructure practitioners own, with evidence anchored to Bitcoin and records readable by AI agents with no vendor in the verification path.

This paper specifies how the two combine into a single stack. OLP is the substrate: identity, records, payments and governance. CREDU is the credentialing layer built on that substrate. Together they form a continuous pipeline from first learning artefact to publicly trusted, agent-procurable professional standing, delivered as free and open-source public-good infrastructure with no central operator who can de-list, gate or extract.

01The problem

The problem

Learning records and professional credentials are held apart, on platforms neither the learner nor the practitioner owns. A learner's evidence of work lives inside an institution's LMS and evaporates on exit. A practitioner's credentials live inside professional-body databases and vendor platforms that can gate, charge or withdraw access. Neither record is readable by the AI agents that will increasingly find, verify and procure professional work.

The result is a broken pipeline: the years of verifiable effort a person accumulates while learning do not carry forward into the record that makes them procurable as a professional. Each stage starts from zero, on someone else's infrastructure.

02One substrate, two layers

The design answer: one substrate, two layers

OLP and CREDU were designed on the same four commitments, which is what makes them composable:

  1. Sovereignty: the record lives on infrastructure the participant owns, from the first learning commit to the last professional attestation.
  2. Bitcoin as the trust root: evidence is anchored to Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps for tamper-evidence; Lightning carries payments. No other chain, no vendor anchor.
  3. No vendor in the verification path: anyone can verify any record using only public infrastructure.
  4. Agents as first-class readers: records are structured for machine reading first; humans receive a rendered view of the same data.

03Shared primitives

Shared primitives

The combination is not an integration project between two systems; it is one architecture expressed at two stages of a person's development. Four primitives are shared end to end.

Identity: one Nostr keypair for life. The keypair a learner receives on joining an OLP cohort is the same identity that later signs their CREDU node's discovery event (kind:30078). Reputation accrues to one key across the whole journey. NIP-05 gives it a human-readable handle; no platform account is ever the identity.

Records: the Git repository that grows up. The OLP learning portfolio and the CREDU professional graph are the same object at different maturities. The learner's repository holds project work, reflections and program completions under version control. When the learner qualifies, the repository is restructured against the CREDU schema (a credu.json manifest plus credentials, CPD, publications and attestations records), deployed to a credu.* URL, and entered into the federation registry. Nothing is migrated off anyone's platform, because it was never on one.

Anchoring: the same proof for homework and for licences. Evidence files in both layers are hashed with SHA-256 and anchored to Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps using credu-cli. A learning artefact anchored in week one of a cohort is verifiable by the same block-explorer query as a professional CPD record anchored a decade later.

Payments: Lightning from tuition to CPD delivery. OLP carries tuition, allocation and timelocked funds on Lightning. CREDU carries payment for CPD delivery on the same rail: attendees pay the deliverer, and no money ever flows for attestation, keeping the reputational incentive clean.

04The pipeline

The learner-to-practitioner pipeline

The combined stack produces a pipeline with no re-registration, no data export and no trust reset at any stage.

  1. Join (OLP): a participant receives a Nostr keypair and forks a Git learning portfolio. Governance participation begins immediately through the SIP process.
  2. Learn (OLP): programs are delivered through the didactic core; work, evidence and reflections are committed and anchored. AI-supported learning tools write adaptive curriculum and reflection records into the same portfolio.
  3. Attest (OLP to CREDU): peer attestations begin inside the cohort. The first peer-attested CPD cross-reference, a session record on the deliverer's graph linked to an entry on the attendee's graph, is a primitive both layers share and no centralised platform offers.
  4. Qualify (CREDU entry gate): on attaining current membership of an IFAC-recognised professional body (or the equivalent gate as CREDU extends to other professions), the participant opens a pull request against nodes.json. Verification lands at one of three tiers depending on what the body publishes: deterministic directory lookup, geographic-only lookup, or member certificate with peer review by a cohort member.
  5. Practise (CREDU): the graph is live at a credu.* URL, exposes llms.txt and a stable JSON schema, and publishes its Nostr discovery event. AI agents can now find, read, verify and procure the practitioner with no login, API key or curated index. The practitioner directs the AI's work, signs for it and carries the liability: the profession governs the AI.
  6. Accumulate (CREDU v3–v4): DID-based signing, W3C Verifiable Credentials, and ultimately client-signed, zero-knowledge proof-of-experience records make the practitioner findable for what they have done, not only what they have learned, with CPD demoted to the entry condition.

Throughout the pipeline, one Nostr keypair signs everything and one Git repository holds everything: the learning portfolio of stages 1 to 3 is the same repository, restructured against the CREDU schema, that becomes the professional graph of stages 4 to 6.

05Governance

Governance and the public-good guarantee

OLP's governance bootstrap (Council, SIP process, multisig treasury) governs the shared substrate. CREDU's registry review distributes from a sole reviewer in v1 to branch protection, org teams, CODEOWNERS and automated status checks in v2, so review scales through the cohort itself. Professional bodies are an entry-point criterion, not the customer; bodies that participate gain early-mover advantage, and over time recognition begins to run through members rather than only top-down through IFAC.

The whole stack is free, open source and openly licensed. Each professional graph is a self-hosted static site and each learning record a self-hosted repository, so the network scales to thousands of participants and their agents at negligible marginal cost, with no central server as a bottleneck and no point at which any operator can de-list, gate or extract. Monetisation sits deliberately outside the protocol: CREDU Academy teaches the technical fluency sovereignty requires, and the optional hosted anchoring service at credu.app runs at cost-recovery.

07Conclusion

Conclusion

Separately, OLP fixes who owns the learning record and CREDU fixes who owns the professional record. Combined, they close the pipeline between the two: one keypair, one growing repository, one trust root, from a learner's first anchored artefact to a practitioner an AI agent can discover, verify and procure.

Be discoverable. Be trusted. Be procurable, on infrastructure you own.
