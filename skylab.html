The problem

School networks are locked down. That's the point, and the problem.

Every school network in Australia is locked down, and rightly so: it carries student records, staff systems and thousands of devices, and it has to be protected. But the same lockdown means students can never touch the technology the real world runs on. No connecting an IoT device. No standing up a VR rig. No running a server, hosting a website from scratch, or seeing what a blockchain node actually does. Students graduate having used devices for years without ever learning how any of it works.

The answer isn't to loosen the school network. It's to build a second, smaller one next to it, with its own connection, its own rules, and its own enterprise-grade safety controls.

The model

A school within a school

SkyLab is a dedicated learning space with its own satellite internet connection, physically and logically separate from school infrastructure.

  • 1
    Its own connection from the skyA Starlink dish on the lab roof means the lab never touches the school's network, filtering or firewall. Nothing in the lab can reach school systems, and nothing students do can affect them.
  • 2
    A hardened, managed gatewayAn enterprise-grade Wi-Fi 7 router (GL.iNet Flint series) configured by us: network segmentation with VLANs, DNS-level filtering and malware blocking, VPN with kill-switch, and full traffic visibility for supervising teachers.
  • 3
    A supervised learning spaceOne room, clearly bounded, and always staffed. Only teachers holding a current Working With Children Check are issued lab credentials, and students work under supervision and a lab-specific acceptable use agreement: real freedom inside a real fence.
  • 4
    Remote support, if you want itWe can monitor, patch and manage the lab remotely, and protect every lab device with managed endpoint protection, so no extra load lands on your IT staff.

Duty of care

Open for learning. Closed to everything else.

"Separate" never means "unsupervised". Every SkyLab is a supervised environment with enterprise-grade safeguards at a fraction of enterprise cost, and only teachers holding a current Working With Children Check are issued credentials.

Physical & logical segregation

No cable, no bridge, no shared credential between SkyLab and school systems. An incident in the lab stays in the lab.

DNS filtering & malware blocking

Gateway-level filtering (AdGuard-class) blocks inappropriate content and known-malicious domains for every device, every time.

Full traffic visibility

Supervising teachers can see what every lab device is doing, live. Freedom with a flight recorder.

Endpoint protection

Optional managed protection on every lab device: the same class of tooling businesses use, taught as part of the learning.

Supervised, credentialed access

Lab time is always staffed. Credentials are issued only to teachers holding a current Working With Children Check, and students and parents sign a lab-specific acceptable use agreement.

Remote management

We can patch, monitor and lock down the lab remotely, and shut the uplink off entirely outside lab hours.

Questions principals ask

FAQ

Does this touch our school network or Department systems at all?
No. That's the entire design. SkyLab has its own internet connection from the sky, its own router, and its own devices. There is no cable, Wi-Fi bridge or shared login between the two networks, so an issue in the lab cannot reach student records or staff systems.
Is a residential Starlink plan allowed for this?
The lab is set up as a standalone residential-style service precisely because it isn't part of the school's ICT estate; it serves one dedicated learning space. We'll walk through the arrangement with you during scoping so the billing and ownership structure suits your school and sector requirements.
What about student safety online?
SkyLab is a supervised environment. Credentials are issued only to teachers holding a current Working With Children Check, the gateway enforces DNS-level filtering and malware blocking for every device, teachers have live visibility of all traffic, and the uplink can be scheduled to switch off outside lab hours. Students also sign a lab-specific acceptable use agreement. It's more observable than a typical home connection, by design.
Who owns the equipment?
Your school does. Hardware is purchased at cost (or you can buy it yourself at retail) and it stays yours. DPHF charges only for setup time and any ongoing support you choose.
What does "blockchain technology education" involve?
It's a networking and distributed-systems exercise, purely about the technology: students see how a global peer-to-peer system synchronises, verifies and stays online, the same skills that underpin every distributed system they'll meet in industry.
What does the school need to provide?
A dedicated room, a roof or wall spot with clear sky view for the dish, a standard power point, and a supervising teacher. We handle the rest.
